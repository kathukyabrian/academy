# Crypto Trading 101

## Phase 1: Fundamentals
### 1. What is Cryptocurrency?
- digital money that exists on a blockchain instead of being issued by a bank or government

|Traditional Money|Cryptocurrency|
|---|---|
|issues by central banks|created according to blockchain protocols|
|banks verify transactions|thousands of computers verify transactions|
|can be frozen by banks|you control your funds through the wallet|
|physical or digital|entirely digital|

- examples:
    - Bitcoin
    - Ethereum
    - Solana
    - Cardano

#### Why was Bitcoin created
- 2009, Satoshi Nakamoto introduced Bitcoin with the goal of creating money that could be transferred directly between people without relying on banks
- idea is a peer-to-peer model

#### What gives Bitcoin Value
- doesn't generate cashflow like a business
- value comes from factors such as:
    - limited supply (capped at 21m BTC)
    - people are willing to buy and hold it
    - it can be transferred globally
    - difficult to counterfeit
    - widespread recognition and adoption

#### Coins vs Tokens
##### Coin
- has its own blockchain
- examples:
    - Bitcoin
    - Ethereum
    - Solana
##### Token
- built on top of an existing blockchain
- example
    ```
    Ethereum Blockchain
        - USDT
        - LINK
        - UNI
        - AAVE      
    ```
- those examples rely on Ethereum's blockchain rather than operating their own.

#### Why Do People Buy Crypto?
- common reasons:
    - long term investing
    - short term trading
    - international transfers
    - decentralized finance
    - collectibles(NFTs)
    - participation in blockchain-based applications

#### Important Terms
##### Bull Market

##### Bear Market

### 2. What is a Blockchain?
- shared digital lender
- think of it like a spreadsheet that
s copied to thousands of computers around the world.
- when a new transaction is added:
    - everyone updates their copy
    - the entries are linked together
    - changing past records  would be difficult
- the computers in a blockchain are called __nodes__

#### What is a Block?
- transactions aren't added one at a time forever
- they are grouped into blocks
- example
```
Block #850

Brian → Alice     1 BTC
Alice → John      0.5 BTC
Mike → Sarah      2 BTC
John → Brian      0.2 BTC
```
- once enough transactions accumulate, they form a block

#### Why its called a Blockchain
```
Block 1
   ↓
Block 2
   ↓
Block 3
   ↓
Block 4
   ↓
Block 5
```
- each block references the one before it
- this creates a __chain of blocks__
- __block + chain = blockchain__

#### What is inside a block?
```
Block #120

Transactions:
-------------
Alice → Brian 2 BTC
John → Sarah 1 BTC

Previous Block Reference

Timestamp

Block Identifier (Hash)
```
- very important field is the hash

#### What is a Hash?
- a digital fingerprint
- example
```
Hello

↓

8b1a9953c4611296a827abf8c47804d7
```

- if you change even a single character the hash changes completely
```
hello

↓

5d41402abc4b2a76b9719d911017c592
```
- this property helps detect any changes to stored data

#### Why someone can't change old transactions
- if someone tries to change a transaction, the block's hash would change
- since the next block references the previous one, the chain no longer fits together

```
Block 10 ✅

↓

Block 11 ❌
(hash mismatch)

↓

Block 12 ❌

↓

Block 13 ❌
```
- to successfully alter history, an attacker would have to recompute the affected block and all following blocks and convince the rest of the network to accept the altered chain

#### How does everyone agree?
- suppose 5k computers receive this transaction
```
Brian sends Alice 1 BTC
```

- each computer checks:
    - does Brian actually own 1BTC?
    - has Brian already spent it elsewhere?
    - is the transaction valid?
- if the transaction passes the network rules, it can be included in a new block
- the process of reaching agreement is called consensus
- different blockchain use different consensus mechanisms.

#### Bitcoin Uses Mining(Proof of Work)
- Bitcoin uses a system called __Proof of Work__
- miners compete to solve a computational puzzle.
- the winner earns the right to add the next block and receives:
    - newly created bitcoin
    - transaction fees included in that block
- mining isn't creating fake money - its the process that helps secure the network and determine who gets to propose the next block

#### Not Every Blockchain uses Mining
- Ethereum used mining until 2022
- today it uses __Proof of Stake__
- here:
    - participants lock stake(some of their crypto)
    - the network selects validators according to its rules to propose and validate new blocks
    - validators can earn rewards for honest participation and may loose some of their stake if they violatethe protocol


#### Why Blockchain is Valuable:
- provides
    - decentralization
    - transparency
    - security
    - high availability


### 3. Wallets(hot vs cold)
#### What is a wallet?
- a wallet doesn't store your crypto
- crypto is recorded on the blockchain
- a wallet stores your cryptographic keys
- it is just a key that proves you're allowed to spend the crypto associated with your address.

#### Public Keys
- example
```
bc1qxy2kgdygjrsqtzq2...
```
- more like your email address
- you can give it to anyone
- people use it to send you crypto

#### Private Keys
- example:
```
L3n8Kd82jS98...
```
- the master secret
- anyone with it can spend your crypto
- __never share your private key__
- when you want to send crypto, your private key is not sent over the network, instead using the private key, a __digital signature__ is created proving that you authorized the transaction
- other nodes can verify the signature using your public key but they cannot derive the private key from it.

#### Seed Phrases
- remembering a long private key would be impractical
- wallets generate sth like:
```
apple
river
planet
yellow
coffee
```
- usually 12, 18 or 24 words
- this is usually your __seed phrase__ aka __recovery phrase__
- from this single phrase, the wallet can regenerate all of your private keys and addresses
- this is more like the __master backup__
- if you loose your phone, you can recover the keys with your seed phrase.

#### Hot Wallets
- connected to the internet
- examples:
    - mobile wallet apps
    - browser extension wallets
    - desktop wallets
##### Pros
- convenient
- fast
- great for everyday use
##### Cons
- more exposed to malware

#### Cold Wallets
- keeps your private keys offline
- a dedicated hardware device
##### Pros
- more resistant to online attacks
- ideal for long-term storage
##### Cons
- costs money
- less convenient for frequent trading

#### Custodial wallets
```
You

↓

Exchange

↓

Private Keys
```  
- the exchange holds the private keys
- you access your account with username, password and 2FA

#### Non-custodial wallets
```
You

↓

Private Keys
```
- only you control the keys
- no company can recover them if you loose them

#### Why exchanges aren't really wallets
- suppose you're making several trades a day.
- you don't want to transfer crypto to and from a hardware wallet every time.
- so active traders often keep only the funds they need for trading on an exchange.
- long-term holdings are commonly moved to a wallet where the owner controls the keys.
- __Trade on the exchange. Save in your own wallet.__

### 4. Exchanges
#### What an exchange does
- brings buyers and sellers together
```
Seller  ───────┐
               │
               ▼
          BINANCE
               ▲
               │
Buyer   ───────┘
```
- doesn't determine crypto prices
- the market does

#### Buyers vs Sellers
- if buyer price and seller price match - the trade completes
- if they don't match - the exchange waits - this wait is what is called an __order book__.

#### Order Books(waiting list)
```
SELLERS

101,500
101,300
101,100
101,000  ← Cheapest Seller


----------------------


99,000   ← Highest Buyer
98,900
98,700
98,500

BUYERS
```
- one side wants to buy
- one side wants to sell

#### Bid and Ask Prices
- __best bid__ - highest buying price
- __best ask__ - lowest selling price

#### Spread
- difference between best ask and best bid
- smaller spread means easier trading
- large spread = less liquid market
- so what exactly is liquidity?

#### Why Prices Move
- the market price is not an average
- its the price of the __most recent completed trade__
```
Seller

"I'll sell for $100,000."

↓

Buyer agrees.

↓

Trade executes.

↓

Market price becomes $100,000.

↓

Another buyer comes I'll pay $100,200.

↓

Trade executes.

↓

Market price becomes $100,200.
```

#### Market Orders
- suppose you want crypto
- you don't care about the exact price
- you click buy - exchange will choose the __best ask__
```
Seller

100,000

↓

You

Market Buy

↓

Trade happens immediately.
```
##### Pros
- instant execution
- very simple
##### Cons
- you don't control the exact execution price
- in fast moving/low liquidity markets the average prices may be worse than expected(__slippage__)

#### Limit Orders
- you limit the bid price eg
> "I'm only buying if Bitcoin drops to $95,000."
- the exchange stores your order in the order book
- you remain in the waiting list until someone agrees to sell at that price

##### Pros
- you choose your price
- useful for planning entries and exits
##### Cons
- your order may never execute

#### Liquidity
- high liquidity means:
    - lots of buyers
    - lots of sellers
    - small spreads
    - easier trading
- Bitcoin and Ethereum are highly liquid

### 5. How Crypto Prices Move
- prices move because buyers and sellers compete with each other.
- every market works on 1 principle:
    > demand and supply
- when demand is higher than supply, prices go up
- the inverse is true

#### What Creates Demand
1. Good News
    - a govt of  a large country announces that banks can now offer Bitcoin services
    - many people will buy
    - demand increases
    - prices increases
1. Bad News
    - eg a major exchange is hacked
    - people become nervous
    - many start selling
    - supply increases
    - prices may fall
1. Fear and Greed
    - markets are driven by human emotion.
    - imagine Bitcoin rises
        ```
        100k

        105k

        110k

        115k
        ```
    - people who ignored it at 100k suddenly think: __I'm missing out__ - this is called __FOMO__
    - they buy
    - this pushes the price even higher
    - now imagine:
        ```
        115k

        110k

        100k

        90k
        ```
    - people panic - sell before it goes to zero
    - this is called __panic selling__
    - results in more selling which can push the prices down further.

1. Whales
    - an entity that owns a very large amount of crypto
    - if a whale buys or sells a very large amount especially in a less liquid market, it can move the price significantly.

1. Volume
    - tells how much was traded over a period.
        ```
        Yesterday

        BTC traded:
        $30 Billion
        ```
    - high volume means:
        - strong participation
        - more confidence in the move

#### Why Don't Prices Stay Stable?
- every second:
    - thousands of new buy orders arrive
    - thousands of sell orders arrive
    - news breaks
    - traders react
    - algorithms trade automatically
- the market is constantly finding a new balance between buyers and sellers

### 6. Spot vs Margin vs Futures
|Spot|Margin|Futures|
|---|---|---|

|Simpler|More complex|Most complex|

### 7. Market Cap and Liquidity
#### Market Cap
- tells the total value of a crypto currency
```
market cap = price * circulating supply

where price = current price of one coin and circulating supply is the number of coins currently in circulation
```
#### Large Cap, Mid Cap, Small Cap
##### Large Cap
- eg Bitcoin, Etherium
- characteristics:
    - more stable
    - high liquidity
    - harder for a single trader to move the price significantly
##### Mid Cap
- characteristics:
    - moderate risk
    - moderate growth potential
    - lower liquidity than large-cap coins
##### Small Cap
- characteristics:
    - can rise 100% quickly
    - can fall 90% quickly
    - lower liquidity
    - higher risk

#### Liquidity
- connection btwn market cap and liquidity:
    - in large cap, there are thousands of buyers and sellers every minute
    - if you buy 0.1BTC, your order barely affects the price

    - in small cap, only a handful of people are trading
    - you try to buy $50,000 worth - there aren't enough sellers near the current price
    - your order pushes the price higher
    - that's low liquidity

#### Market Cap vs Liquidity
imagine
```
Coin A
Market Cap: $5b
Very active trading every day
High Liquidity
```

```
Coin B
Market Cap: $5b
Very little daily trading
High Liquidity
```
- liquidity depends on how actively people are buying and selling.

#### Why Traders love Liquid Markets:
Suppose Bitcoin is trading around $100,000.

You decide:

> "Sell now."

Within seconds, someone buys it.

Easy.

Now imagine an obscure token with very few buyers.

You click "Sell."

Nobody wants it at your price.

You may need to lower your asking price to find a buyer.

#### Why Penny Coins Are Dangerous
You've probably seen videos saying:

> "This coin is only $0.00001."

People think:

> "If it reaches $1, I'll become a millionaire."

But let's do the math.

Suppose:

```
Supply = 500 billion coins

At $1, the market cap would be:

500 billion × $1

=

$500 billion

```
Now ask yourself:

Is it realistic for this project to become one of the largest crypto networks in the world?

Sometimes the answer is no.

That's why market cap matters more than the price of one coin.

#### What do professional traders look at?
When evaluating a coin, they often ask:

- What's the market cap?
- What's the daily trading volume?
- How liquid is it?
- Who is buying?
- Is there enough activity to enter and exit positions easily?

Notice that "What's the price?" isn't the first question.

### 8. Stable Coins
#### What is a Stable Coin
- a cryptocurrency whose value is designed to stay close to another asset usually the __USD__
- example
```
1 USDT ≈ $1

1 USDC ≈ $1
```

- unlike Bitcoin
```
Morning

$100,000

Evening

$97,000
```
- USDT tries to __stay__ around:
```
Morning

$1.00

Evening

$1.00
```
- StableCoins are designed to maintain their peg but small deviations can occur

#### Why StableCoins exist
- imagine:
```
You buy BTC at $100,000.

Later it rises to $110,000.

You decide to sell.

Question:

After selling, where should your money go?
```
- Without stablecoins:
```
BTC

↓

Cash in your bank
```
- that would require bank transfers every time you entered or exited a trade - slow, expensive, not practical

- With Stable Coins
```
BTC

↓

USDT

↓

BTC

↓

ETH

↓

SOL
```
- everything stays on the blockchain and within the exchange ecosystem - much faster
- just think of the same as digital dollars
- when you see __BTC/USDT__ it means, how many USDT does it take to buy 1 BTC

#### Why traders use USDT instead of USD
- with stablecoins suppose Bitcoin starts crashing, you don't need to withdraw money to your bank - you can sell your Bitcoin for USDT
```
BTC

↓

USDT
```
- now, your value is no longer tied to Bitcoin's price movement
- when you're ready, you can use that USDT to buy another asset.

#### The difference between USDT and USDC
|USDT(Tether)|USDC|
|---|---|
|Most widely traded stablecoin|Also aims to stay close to $1|
|Supported on almost every exchange|Widely used by traders and institutions|
|Often has the highest trading volume|Available on many major blockchains|

#### What a trading pair means
- examples
```
BTC/USDT - buy or sell Bitcoin using USDT
ETH/BTC - buy or sell Ethereum using Bitcoin
SOL/USDT - buy or sell Solana using USDT
```

#### Why traders "park" their money in stablecoins
- example:
    - you deposit KES 100,000
    - journey might look like this:
        ```
        KES

        ↓

        USDT

        ↓

        BTC

        ↓

        Profit

        ↓

        USDT
        ```
    - most traders end their trade back in a stable coin, ready for the next opportunity


### 9. Gas Fees
#### What are gas fees?
- fee you pay to the blockchain network to process your transaction
- you're not paying Binance(wallet)
- you're paying the network participants(miners or validators) who process and secure the transaction

#### Why do gas fees exist?
- create a cost for using the network  helping discourage spam while also rewardig miners and validators

#### Why do different blockchains have different fees?
- every blockchain has its own design

##### Bitcoin
- built for:
    - security
    - reliability
    - decentralization
- not primarily for speed
- typical transaction times are measured in minutes and fees may vary depending on network demand.

##### Ethereum
- does much more than transfer money
- it also runs __smart contracts__ eg:
    - games
    - decentralized exchanges
    - lending platforms
    - NFT marketplaces
- more demand = higher fees

##### Solana
- designed with much higher throughput
- it can process many transactions per second than Bitcoin, Ethereum
- fees are often low

#### Why Ethereum is sometimes expensive
- it has high traffic
- people start offering higher fees to get priority
```
User A

$1

User B

$2

User C

$5

User D

$10
```
- higher paying transactions are generally processed sooner

```
Invest $1000

```

- imagine a tiny coin
```
Price: $0.01
```
- a group of people start posting:
```

```
- a thousand of people rush in
- price becomes $0.10
- the original organizers sell into that buying pressure.
- price crashes to $0.02
- late buters are left with  losses

- Imagine someone launches a new project.

- Amazing.
- Marketing:
- Amazing.

- One morning...
- The developers disappear.
- The project's funds are drained.

### Support and Resistance
- imagine a bouncing ball
- drop a ball
- it hits the floor
- it bounces
- in trading, the floor is called __Support__
- now throw the ball upward
- it hits the ceiling, it falls
- the ceiling is called __resistance__

#### What is Support?
- a price area where __buyers have historically been strong enough to stop or slow a decline__
- example:
    ```
    120k

    115k

    110k

    105k

    100k  ← Buyers step in

    104k

    109k
    ```
- as price reached 100k buyers entered aggressively.
- the market bounced
- so traders begin thinking:
    > 100k looks like an important level

#### What is Resistance?
- imagine
```
100k

105k

110k

115k

120k ← Sellers step in

116k

112k
```
- price reached 120k
- many people sold
- price fell
- now traders think
    > 120k is an important resistance

#### Why does this happen?
- here's where psychology comes in.
- imagine you bought Bitcoin at $100,000.
- it rises to $120,000.
- you think:
> "Great! I'll sell and take profit."
- now imagine thousands of traders had the same idea.
- lots of sell orders appear around $120,000.
- that creates __resistance__.
---
- now imagine someone missed buying Bitcoin at $100,000.
- price shoots to $120,000.
- eventually it falls back.
- when it reaches $100,000 again, they think:
> "I missed this opportunity before. I'm buying this time."
- many buyers do the same.
- support forms.
- support and resistance __are not magical lines__
- they're areas where lots of traders make similar decisions

#### Why they're areas not lines:
- many beginners draw this:
```
────────── 100,000
```
- professional traders think more like this:
```
99,500

████████████

100,500
```
- price doesn't have to reverse at exactly 100,000
- it might react anywhere in that region
- that's why experienced traders talk about __support zones__ and __resistance zones__

#### Price Doesn't Always Bounce
- support doesn't guarantee a bounce
- resistance does not guarantee a reversal
- sometimes support fails
```
120k

115k

110k

105k

100k  ← Support

99k

95k

90k
```
- buyers weren't strong enough
- the market kept falling

#### What is a Breakout?
- suppose resistance is at 120k
- price reaches it several times
```
120k ❌

118k

120k ❌

117k

120k ❌
```
- then one day:
```
120k

122k

125k

130k
```
- price breaks through
- that's called a breakout
- reason: buyers finally overwhelmed the sellers around that area

#### Support Becomes Resistance
- suppose Bitcoin has support at 100k
- eventually it breaks below:
```
105k

100k

95k

90k
```
- months later, price rises again:
```
90k

95k

100k
```
- instead of continuing higher, it falls
- why?
    - many traders who bought at 100k and watched the price fall think:
    > "If I can just get my money back, I'll sell."
    - they create selling pressure around the old support
    - now the old support acts as resistance

#### Resistance Becomes Support
- price breaks above resistance
- later it comes back to test it
- instead of falling through, buyers step in
- the old resistance becomes new support
- this is called __role reversal__
- professional traders watch for this all the time

#### How do traders find support?
- they look for places where price repeatedly reacted.
- example:
    ```
    120k

    110k

    100k ← Bounce

    110k

    100k ← Bounce

    108k

    100k ← Bounce
    ```

#### Strong vs Weak Support
- not all support levels are the same

##### Weak Support
- price touched it once
```
100k

↓

Bounce
```

##### Strong Support
```
100k

Bounce

100k

Bounce

100k

Bounce

100k

Bounce
```
### Candlesticks
- every candlestick answers one question
    - __who won the battle during this period, buyer/seller?__
- basically a summary of the battle

#### 4 aspects
- open
- high
- low
- close

- example:
```
Open  = 100
High  = 110
Low   = 98
Close = 108
```

#### Bullish Candle
```
Open = 100

↓

102

↓

105

↓

108

↓

110

↓

Close = 109
```
- buyers won
- the candle closes higher than it opened

#### Bearish Candle
```
Open = 110

↓

108

↓

105

↓

102

↓

Close = 100
```
- sellers won

#### The Body
- distance between the open and the close
- large body:
```
Open = 100

Close = 120
```
- small body:
```
Open = 100

Close = 101
```

#### The Wickes tell a Story
- imagine this:
```
High = 120

Open = 100

```
- price reached 120
- it didn't stay there
- sellers pushed it down
- upper wick tells us:
    - buyers reached 120 but couldn't hold it.
- now imagine:
```
Open = 110

Low = 90

Close = 108
```
- price dropped to 90
- buyers pushed back to 108
- the long lower wick tells us:
> sellers were strong for a while but buyers fought back

#### A candle in the middle of nowhere
- imagine this:
```
140

135

130

125

120

115
```
- a Hammer forms at 127.
- should you buy? You don't know.
- why?
    - there's no context.
    - no support.
    - no resistance.
- the hammer might mean very little by itself.

- lets add context:
```
140

130

120

110

100  ← Strong Support
```
- price falls
```
140

130

120

100
```
- at 100, a Hammer appears.
- now traders become interested.
- why?
    - buyers previously defended 100.
    - buyers are defending it again.
    - the candle shows they regained control before the close.
- the same candlestick means much more because of where it formed.

#### Types of Candles
##### Hammer
- small body
- long lower wick
- little or no upper wick
```
Buyers

↓

Sellers attack

↓

Price falls

↓

Buyers fight back

↓

Price closes near the top
```
- buyers rejected lower prices
- most meaningful after a decline

##### Shooting Star
- small body
- long upper wick
```
Buyers attack

↓

Price rises

↓

Sellers fight back

↓

Price closes near the bottom
```

##### Doji
- open and close are almost the same
```
Buyers pushed.

↓

Sellers pushed.

↓

Neither side clearly won.

↓

The market is undecided.
```
- after a strong trend, that indecision can be important—but on its own, a Doji isn't a buy or sell signal.

#### Strong vs Weak Bullish Candle
```
Candle A

Open = 100

Close = 101
```
- not very impressive

```
Candle B

Open = 100

Close = 118
```
- buyers dominated
- much stronger

- not all bullish candles carry the same weight

#### Reading a Sequence
```
Bullish

Bullish

Bullish

Small Candle

Bearish
```
- buyers were in control.
- momentum started slowing.
- sellers finally pushed back.

#### Professional Mindset
- when a trader sees a candle,they don't ask
> "Is this a hammer?"

- they ask:
    - where did it form?
    - what trend is the market in?
    - is it near support?
    - is it near resistance?
    - is there strong trading volume?
    - does the next candle confirm the story?
### Market Structure
- support and resistance tell you where the market reacts
- __market structure__ tells you what the market is trying to do

#### Higher High
- the market reaches a new peak higher than the previous peak
- example:
```
120
125
130
```
- each peak is higher - this is bullish

#### Higher Low
- markets don't move straight up
- they rise
- then pull back
- then rise again
```
130

125 -> pullback

140
```
- pullback stopped at 125 which is higher than the previous low
- that's a higher low
- buyers stepped in earlier than before

#### Uptrend
- every new peak is higher
- every new dip is higher
- that's a healthy uptrend

#### Lower High
- price tries to rise
- but can't reach the previous peak
- example:
```
130

125

120
```
- the new high is lower
- indicates that buyers may be loosing strength

#### Lower Low
- price falls
- recovers a little
- then falls even further
```
100
95
90
```
- each new low is lower than the previous one
- that's bearish

#### A Downtrend
- every new low is lower
- every new dip is lower
- sellers remain in control

#### Break of Structure
- happens when price breaks an important swing point

```
Uptrend

HH

HL

HH

HL

Break Below HL
```
- the market is no longer making Higher Lows
- the structure has changed
- doesn't automatically mean a downtrend has begun - but it tells traders to pay attention

#### Why Market Structure Matters
- imagine 2 trader
##### Trader A
- buys because
> "The candle is green"

##### Trader B
- buys because:
    - market is making higher highs
    - market is making higher lows
    - price just bounced from support
    - buyers defended the level

- professional traders wait for a breakout before becoming aggressive.
